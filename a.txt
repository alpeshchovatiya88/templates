Back-testing compares model predictions, including model build and calibration, against actual experience to assess whether predicted losses and claims remain reasonably aligned with observed outcomes. Over the 2009–2015 back-testing period, SMI3.0 demonstrated a conservative overall bias by overpredicting both losses and claims. Predicted losses were $519.6M compared with actual losses of $487.6M, representing an overprediction of $32.0M (6.6%). The predicted loss ratio (LR) was 17.21% compared with an actual LR of 16.55%. Annual loss results showed mixed performance, with the largest overprediction occurring in 2015 ($21.5M), followed by 2011 ($9.9M), while losses were underpredicted in 2009 ($7.0M). Regionally, losses were overpredicted in Rest of Ontario ($26.7M) and Manitoba ($13.4M), while Quebec experienced the largest underprediction ($17.8M). Claims back-testing also demonstrated a conservative bias, with 7,998 predicted claims compared with 6,915 actual claims, representing an overprediction of 1,083 claims (15.7%). Claims were slightly underpredicted in 2009 but overpredicted in all subsequent years, with the largest differences occurring in 2015 (331 claims) and 2011 (230 claims).

The LR back-testing corridor assessment indicates that annual predicted loss ratios generally remained within the ±20% corridor around actual experience, with a notable exception in 2015, when the predicted LR of 11.29% exceeded the actual LR of 8.19% by approximately 37.9%. The 2015 breach is assessed as a low-severity exception rather than conclusive evidence of a model-fitness issue, considering the model’s generally conservative aggregate back-testing results across the 2009–2015 period and its approved production use for file-level underwriting risk ranking and RBP rather than absolute loss forecasting or scenario-based loss projection. This assessment is further supported by extensive business and underwriting validation, stable rank-ordering evidence from challenger, seed, sensitivity, HPI timing, and HPO tests, and documented use restrictions requiring ongoing monitoring of data and model performance.
