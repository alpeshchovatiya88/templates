What the data shows

The strongest pattern is that SMI3 generally overestimates the Actual LR, especially for the TS series.

For TS, the gap is very large in the early years. For example, in 2009, SMI3 LR is about 46.7% compared with an Actual LR of only 19.4%, a difference of roughly 27.3 percentage points. The gap peaks around 2010 at 30.3 points and remains substantial through 2013.

The gap then narrows through 2014–2017, but interestingly widens again in 2018 and 2019. In 2019, for example, SMI3 TS is about 23.5%, while Actual TS is only 0.65%, creating a gap of approximately 22.9 points.

80/20 performs closer to Actual

The 80/20 approach appears substantially closer to Actual LR than TS for most book years.

In the early years, it still overestimates Actual—for example, 2009 is 33.2% SMI3 versus 19.5% Actual—but the differences are generally smaller than under TS.

The fit becomes particularly close in 2017–2019. In 2019, SMI3 80/20 is about 3.42% versus Actual at 0.94%, only about a 2.48 percentage-point difference.

There is, however, some deterioration in 2021, where the 80/20 difference rises again to about 7.13 points.

Overall interpretation

If the purpose of this analysis is to assess which SMI3 methodology better approximates the Actual LR, the screenshot strongly suggests that SMI3 80/20 is the better-performing estimate.
