The validation team reviewed the data cleansing and transformation procedures, including duplicate treatment, missing-value handling, outlier controls, data integration, and population construction.

Data quality and record-level controls: The raw policy and borrower extracts contained approximately 6.30 million and 10.29 million records, respectively. Policy preprocessing identified duplicate applications and resulted in 6.28 million cleaned policy records. Repeated ETD claim/delinquency episodes were aggregated to the application level. Following policy transformations and MI/LRUPLUS population construction, the processed policy population contained 2.52 million records. Subsequent integration with the cleaned borrower data resulted in approximately 2.49 million policy-borrower records. Record-count checks support that duplicate removal, aggregation, and merging were performed at the appropriate stages.

Missing-value treatment: Missingness was assessed before and after preprocessing. Missing values were identified across several required policy and borrower fields and were addressed through the prescribed removal or treatment rules. Post-processing checks showed no remaining missing values in the reviewed training and testing model fields. Limited residual missingness was observed in certain fields within the recent population.

Outlier and range treatment: Continuous variables were subject to predefined clipping limits. The highest training-data breach rates were observed for GDSR (0.91%), TDSR (0.66%), and total income (0.21%), while other reviewed variables had relatively low or no breaches. The same clipping framework was applied across development datasets, supporting consistent treatment of extreme values.

Business-rule transformations and population construction: The preprocessing pipeline also applies model-specific eligibility rules, MI/LRUPLUS population construction, claim/delinquency aggregation, credit-data consolidation, feature derivation, and economic-data integration. The resulting populations contained 458,891 training, 317,170 testing, and 74,729 recent observations.

Validation conclusion: Based on the code review and quantitative checks, the data cleansing and transformation process is generally systematic and traceable. The reviewed controls appropriately address duplicates, missing values, extreme observations, and population construction. Business-rule and feature-selection decisions should be explicitly linked to their supporting business rationale or methodology documentation to further strengthen traceability.
