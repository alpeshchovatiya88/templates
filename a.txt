The baseline model was developed using BOOKYEAR 2009–2015. Based on the development documentation reviewed, pre-2009 observations were excluded due to several data and representativeness considerations. The DMS monthly snapshot history used to track policy activity became available from 2009 onward, making pre-2009 policy and delinquency histories less reliable and requiring additional assumptions. Pre-2009 applications also relied on legacy FICO 2 credit scores that were not incorporated into subsequent credit-score updates, creating additional concerns regarding comparability with the current population. In addition, the development team excluded observations associated with the Global Financial Crisis period to reduce the influence of an extreme economic stress episode that was not considered representative of the intended long-run modelling population.

Observations after 2015 were excluded from the baseline training period primarily due to target maturity and data-quality considerations. Bookyears from 2016 onward had less seasoning when the COVID-19 pandemic occurred, and delinquency/claim outcomes were affected by government and lender support programs, including mortgage deferrals and CERB. As a result, the development documentation identifies the later-bookyear target information as potentially incomplete and biased for model-training purposes. The selected 2009–2015 period therefore provides mature bookyears with at least four years of seasoning under comparatively stable economic conditions. A challenger analysis using an expanded 2009–2020 development window was also performed; the supporting analysis indicates similar comparative model performance and provides additional evidence that extending the historical development window does not materially change the principal modelling conclusion.
