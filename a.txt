Data Timeliness: The development population consists of 458,891 observations covering BOOKYEAR 2009–2015, while the testing population contains 317,170 observations. A recent population of 74,729 observations covering EFFECT_SID 202510–202605 was also assessed to evaluate the continued relevance of the development data to the current portfolio. The development sample spans multiple book years and captures variation in portfolio volume and observed bad rates over time, while the recent sample provides sufficiently current data for validation purposes.

Data Representativeness: The development population contains 12,938 bad and 445,953 good observations, corresponding to an overall bad rate of approximately 2.82%, with no missing target values. The population provides broad geographic representation across the reported economic regions, with the largest concentrations in Toronto, Alberta, Quebec, British Columbia, and Rest of Ontario. Review of the key numerical and categorical model variables also indicates meaningful variation in both population distributions and observed bad rates across risk segments, supporting the representativeness of the development sample for model development.

Conclusion: Overall, the development data provide adequate temporal, geographic, and risk-characteristic coverage. The availability of separate testing and recent populations further supports assessment of the model on out-of-development and current business data. Changes between the development and recent populations are evaluated separately through the PSI and data drift analysis in Section 2.1.4.
