The development population contains 12,938 bad and 445,953 good observations, corresponding to an overall bad rate of approximately 2.82%, with no missing target values. The population also provides broad geographic coverage across the reported economic regions, with the largest concentrations in Toronto, Alberta, Quebec, British Columbia, and Rest of Ontario. Review of the key numerical and categorical model variables further indicates meaningful variation in both population composition and observed bad rates across risk segments.

From an independent validation perspective, we find the development population sufficiently representative for model development, given its temporal coverage, geographic breadth, event/non-event representation, and variation across key risk characteristics. We also find the use of separate testing and recent populations appropriate for assessing model performance outside the development period and against more current business conditions. The challenger analysis provides additional support that the selected development population remains reasonable for the intended modelling purpose.

Conclusion: Overall, we find the development data appropriate from a timeliness and representativeness perspective. Changes between the development and recent populations are assessed separately through the PSI and data drift analysis in Section 2.1.4.
